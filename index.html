01 / WHAT IS IT?

A SWITCH.
AN AMPLIFIER.

One semiconductor device. Two fundamental jobs. Billions of them working together can become a processor.

01

CONTROL

A small electrical signal can control a much larger current. That makes the transistor a controllable electronic switch.

02

AMPLIFY

In the right circuit, a transistor can use a small input signal to control a larger output signal.

03

COMPUTE

Transistors can be combined into logic gates. Huge groups of gates form the digital circuits inside computers.

02 / HOW IT WORKS

THREE
TERMINALS.

For a common bipolar junction transistor (BJT), the three terminals are the Base, Collector, and Emitter.

BBASEControl input
CCOLLECTORControlled-current side
EEMITTERCurrent output/reference side
SIGNAL FLOW // SIMPLIFIED
INPUT→BASE→CONTROL→OUTPUT

Think of the base as the control point. A small change there can control current flowing through the transistor's other terminals.

03 / TYPES

NPN vs PNP

Both are BJTs, but their semiconductor structure and current directions differ.

NPN

COMMON LOW-SIDE SWITCH

Often used when a control signal turns a load on by allowing current to flow toward ground.

INPUT HIGH → TRANSISTOR ON
PNP

COMMON HIGH-SIDE SWITCH

Often used when a control signal controls a load connected toward the positive supply.

INPUT LOW → TRANSISTOR ON
TIP: Real transistor circuits depend on biasing, voltage, current, load, and the specific device. This page keeps the first concepts intentionally simple.
