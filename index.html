02 / HOW IT WORKS

THREE
TERMINALS.

For a common bipolar junction transistor (BJT), the three terminals are the Base, Collector, and Emitter.

BBASEControl input
CCOLLECTORControlled-current side
EEMITTERCurrent output/reference side
SIGNAL FLOW // SIMPLIFIED
INPUT→BASE→CONTROL→OUTPUT

A small control signal at the base can control a larger current through the other terminals. That is the basic idea behind transistor switching and amplification.

03 / TYPES

NPN vs PNP

Both are BJTs, but their semiconductor structure and current directions differ.

NPN

LOW-SIDE SWITCH

Often used when a control signal turns a load on by allowing current to flow toward ground.

INPUT HIGH → TRANSISTOR ON
PNP

HIGH-SIDE SWITCH

Often used when a control signal controls a load connected toward the positive supply.

INPUT LOW → TRANSISTOR ON
TIP: Real transistor circuits depend on biasing, voltage, current, load and the specific device. This page keeps the first concepts intentionally simple.
